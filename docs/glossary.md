# Glossary

This glossary explains the tea, coffee, shopping, and storefront terms used across Gray Cup.

### Arabica

Arabica is a coffee species commonly associated with a smoother, more aromatic cup. When choosing between coffees, look for Arabica if you prefer flavour complexity and tasting notes such as chocolate, citrus, or caramel.

### Arabica–Robusta Ratio

An Arabica–Robusta ratio shows how much of each coffee species is used in a blend, such as 70/30 or 95/5. A higher Robusta proportion can contribute more body and intensity, while more Arabica generally emphasizes aroma and flavour detail.

### Black Coffee

Black coffee is coffee served without milk or other additions. Choose a coffee with tasting notes you enjoy, then try it black first so you can notice its natural flavour.

### Blend

A blend combines ingredients from more than one source, variety, or coffee profile. Gray Cup uses “Blend” for products created to provide a particular balance rather than representing one single origin.

### Brew Method

A brew method is the way coffee or tea is prepared, such as filter brewing or another manual method. Match the grind and product type to your equipment, and follow a consistent recipe so you can adjust taste deliberately.

### Buy via Fast

“Buy via Fast” is a shopping route available on the Gray Cup storefront. Use it when you want to move through product selection and purchasing with fewer steps.

### Coffee Finder

The coffee finder is Gray Cup’s recommendation tool for choosing coffee. Answer its questions about your preferences, then use the suggested product, alternatives, and brew note as a starting point rather than a fixed rule.

### Coffee Sample Pack

A coffee sample pack contains several smaller coffee packs so you can compare different products before buying a larger bag. Gray Cup lists sample options containing three, five, or seven 75-gram packs, as well as a build-your-own sampler.

### Custom Blend

A custom blend lets you choose or request a coffee combination instead of selecting only a preset blend. Check the available Arabica–Robusta options and select the combination that suits your preferred strength and flavour.

### Darjeeling Tea

Darjeeling tea comes from the Darjeeling tea-growing region and is often valued for its distinctive, aromatic character. Gray Cup lists Giddapahar Darjeeling Orthodox as a single-origin tea, so check its product details for the available size and preparation guidance.

### Filter Coffee

Filter coffee is prepared by passing hot water through ground coffee and a filter. Use a grind suited to your filter equipment, and adjust the coffee amount, water, or brew time if the cup tastes too weak or strong.

### Free-Delivery Threshold

The free-delivery threshold is the order value above which delivery is free. On Gray Cup, the homepage states that orders above ₹400 qualify, so check your cart total before checkout.

### Ground Coffee

Ground coffee consists of roasted coffee beans that have already been milled for brewing. Select the grind that matches your equipment when a product offers grind choices, because grind size affects extraction and taste.

### Grind

Grind describes how finely or coarsely coffee beans have been ground. Choose a grind suitable for your brewing equipment; if your coffee tastes unusually bitter or thin, changing the grind can help correct the extraction.

### Orthodox Tea

Orthodox tea is made using traditional tea-processing methods that shape and preserve the tea leaves. When buying an orthodox tea, use the product’s origin and preparation information to decide how you want to brew it.

### Product Feed

A product feed is a structured file containing catalogue information for products. Gray Cup exposes a Google product feed at `/feeds/products.xml`, while shoppers should use the product pages for descriptions, choices, and purchasing.

### Product Variant

A product variant is a purchasable version of a product, such as a different pack size or configuration. Review the available variants on the product page and select the one that matches the quantity and format you want.

### Robusta

Robusta is a coffee species often used to add body, intensity, and strength to a blend. Compare the Arabica–Robusta ratio when choosing between Gray Cup blends with different proportions.

### Roast

Roast describes how coffee beans are heated before grinding and brewing. Use roast information together with the product’s tasting notes and your brewing method when deciding whether a coffee suits your preferences.

### Single Origin

Single-origin coffee or tea comes from one defined origin rather than being combined across multiple origins. Choose a single-origin product when you want to explore the characteristics associated with a particular estate or region.

### Specialty Coffee

Specialty coffee generally refers to coffee selected and presented with attention to origin, processing, variety, and flavour quality. Gray Cup’s Specialty Coffee navigation helps shoppers explore coffees using details such as estate, processing method, variety, and tasting notes.

### Tasting Notes

Tasting notes are descriptive words used to suggest flavours or aromas perceived in a coffee, such as chocolate, citrus, lemon zest, cacao nibs, caramel, or toasted notes. Treat them as guidance for what you may notice, not as added ingredients or a guarantee of an identical taste.

### Tea Blend

A tea blend combines teas or ingredients to create a particular flavour profile. Gray Cup describes Dooars & Assam Tea as a blend, so choose it when you want a composed profile rather than a tea identified as one single origin.

### Washed Process

The washed process removes the coffee cherry’s outer fruit skin and sticky pulp with water before the beans are dried. Gray Cup identifies coffees such as Karadykan Estate Coffee and Biccode Estate Coffee as washed, which gives you useful processing context when comparing products.

### WebMCP

WebMCP is a storefront interface that allows compatible browser-based AI agents to use tools such as product search, coffee recommendations, product details, and cart management. It helps an agent use Gray Cup after reaching the site, but it is not a replacement for ordinary search discovery or the website’s normal shopping experience.
