Malgudi, a brand under Continental Coffee, is a widely available South Indian filter coffee sold at the budget end of the category - often described simply as "Continental filter coffee" or "Malgudi filter coffee" in stores. Budget-tier filter coffee brands like this are a useful comparison point for understanding what chicory does to price and taste.

## Where Malgudi Sits

Budget South Indian filter coffee brands, as a category, tend to lean chicory-forward. Chicory is a cheaper input than coffee, it thickens the decoction and gives it body even at lower coffee content, and it produces a mellower, less bitter cup - all of which supports a lower shelf price. This is a well-known category pattern for budget filter coffee generally, not a specific published ratio claim about Malgudi's formulation, which the brand doesn't fully disclose.

For a lot of households, that's exactly the appeal: an affordable, thick, familiar decoction for everyday drinking.

## Where Gray Cup Sits

Gray Cup treats chicory as optional rather than a cost lever - you choose whether you want it and how much:

- **Chicory-optional** - go pure Arabica-Robusta if you want more coffee character, or pick a chicory blend if you specifically want that thicker, mellower cup.
- **Custom ratio** - dial in anywhere from a high-Arabica blend to a traditional 70:30 mix.
- **Roasted to order** - small batches, not sitting in long retail distribution.

## Which One Fits You

If you specifically like a chicory-heavy, budget-friendly filter coffee and don't need to adjust the ratio, a brand like Malgudi serves that need well and is easy to find. If you want to choose exactly how much chicory (if any) goes into your blend, or want a fresher roast, Gray Cup's custom approach is built for that. The chicory-forward budget segment and the custom-ratio segment are simply solving for different things.
