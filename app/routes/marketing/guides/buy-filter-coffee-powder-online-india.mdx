Buying filter coffee online is genuinely easier than buying it off a shop shelf, for one simple reason: you can actually read the blend ratio and roast date before you buy, instead of guessing from a sealed packet. That advantage is wasted if you don't know what to look for, though - so here's what actually matters.

## The Arabica:Robusta Ratio

This is the single biggest factor in how a filter coffee will taste, and reputable listings state it clearly (70:30, 80:20, 90:10, and so on).

- **Higher Arabica** - smoother, more aromatic, lighter body, less caffeine.
- **Higher Robusta** - stronger, more bitter, thicker crema-like body, more caffeine.
- If a listing doesn't state the ratio anywhere, that's a reason to look elsewhere - it's basic information any genuine filter coffee brand should disclose.

## Roast Date, Not Just "Best Before"

A best-before date tells you when coffee stops being safe or reasonable to drink; it doesn't tell you how fresh it is right now. Ground coffee loses aroma within weeks of roasting, so a pack roasted three months ago and one roasted three days ago can carry the same best-before date and taste completely different. Look specifically for a **roast date** on the pack or listing - if a seller only ever shows a best-before date, that's worth noting.

## Whole Bean vs Pre-Ground

- **Whole bean** stays fresher for longer since the surface area exposed to air is much smaller, but only makes sense if you have a grinder capable of a filter-fine grind (not every home grinder gets fine enough).
- **Pre-ground for filter** is the more practical default for most South Indian filter coffee brewing, ground specifically to the size a two-tumbler filter needs - just buy in smaller quantities more often, since it loses aroma faster than whole bean.

## Chicory or Not

Some filter coffee blends include chicory (typically 10-30%), which:

- Thickens the decoction and mellows bitterness.
- Cuts overall caffeine content.
- Gives the classic South Indian "degree coffee" flavour many grew up on.

If you've never tried a chicory blend, it's worth trying a smaller pack before committing to it as your everyday coffee, since it's a genuinely different flavour profile from a pure Arabica-Robusta blend, not just a milder version of it.

## Filter Coffee Brands and Comparing Online

India has a number of long-established filter coffee brands, many with decades of regional reputation, and plenty of newer roasters as well. That's a genuine strength of buying online - you can pull up several sellers' blend ratios and roast dates side by side rather than picking whichever bag happens to be on the shelf. Nothing here is meant to steer you away from established brands; the point is simply that once ratio and freshness are visible, comparing is easy and worth doing regardless of which brand you land on.

## A Quick Buying Checklist

- Ratio stated clearly (Arabica:Robusta, or with chicory percentage)
- Recent roast date, not just a best-before date
- Ground for filter (unless you're set up to grind whole bean fine enough yourself)
- Pack size you'll actually get through in 2-3 weeks
