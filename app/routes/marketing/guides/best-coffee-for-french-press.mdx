The French press (or plunger pot) is full-immersion brewing: coarse grounds steep in hot water for about four minutes, then a metal mesh separates them. It makes a heavy, rich, textured cup with more body than paper-filtered coffee. Because the contact time is long, you want a coffee that stays pleasant over four minutes and doesn't turn harsh.

## What Works in a French Press

- **Coarse grind** - like sea salt. Fine grinds over-extract and slip through the mesh as sludge.
- **Medium to medium-dark roast** - enough body to match the method; very light roasts can taste thin.
- **Fresh beans** - staleness shows badly in a press.
- **1:15 ratio, 4 minutes, water at 93-96 C.** Press slowly, decant fully so it doesn't keep brewing.

## Quick Recipe

1. 30 g coarse coffee, 450 g water at ~94 C.
2. Pour, stir the crust at 1 minute, put the lid on.
3. At 4 minutes, press slowly and pour it all out.
