Black coffee is just coffee and hot water - no milk, no sugar. Any of these four methods makes a good cup in about five minutes. The constants: fresh coffee, water at 90-96 C (just off the boil), and roughly a 1:15 to 1:16 ratio (about 15 g coffee to 240 ml water).

## 1. South Indian Steel Filter

1. Add 2-3 tablespoons of fine coffee to the top chamber, press level with the tamper.
2. Pour in water just off the boil, cover.
3. Wait 15-20 minutes for the decoction to drip through.
4. Dilute the decoction with hot water to taste - roughly 1:2 for black.

## 2. French Press

1. 15 g coarse coffee per cup into the pot.
2. Add 240 ml water at ~94 C, stir, put the lid on.
3. At 4 minutes, press slowly and pour it all out.

## 3. Pour Over

1. Rinse a paper filter, add 15 g medium-ground coffee.
2. Bloom with 30 g water, wait 40 seconds.
3. Pour slowly to 240 g total; aim to finish around 3 minutes.

## 4. Saucepan (No Equipment)

1. Bring 250 ml water almost to a boil, take it off the heat.
2. Stir in 1.5 tablespoons of coffee.
3. Wait 4 minutes, then pour through a fine strainer.

## Fixing a Bad Cup

Bitter? Grind coarser or use cooler water. Sour or weak? Grind finer or brew a little longer. Flat and papery? The coffee is stale - buy fresher and grind just before brewing.
