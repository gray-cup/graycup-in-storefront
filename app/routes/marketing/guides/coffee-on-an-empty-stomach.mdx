The claim that morning coffee before food "spikes cortisol" and "wrecks your gut" gets repeated a lot online. The evidence is thinner than the confidence. For most healthy people, a black coffee before breakfast is fine. This isn't medical advice, but here's the honest picture.

## The Cortisol Claim

Cortisol naturally peaks 30-45 minutes after you wake. The idea that coffee on top of that peak is harmful, or that you should wait 90 minutes, comes from small studies on caffeine and cortisol - and the cortisol response to caffeine shrinks fast as you build tolerance. For a habitual coffee drinker, the effect is minor. There's no good evidence it causes any lasting harm.

## The Stomach Claim

Coffee does stimulate stomach acid and gut motility. For most people that's harmless (and the motility is why coffee "gets things moving"). But if you have:

- **Acid reflux or GERD**
- **Gastritis or an ulcer**
- **A sensitive stomach that gets queasy or shaky on empty-stomach caffeine**

...then eating something first genuinely helps. Otherwise it's preference.

## Sensible Approach

- **No symptoms?** Drink your morning coffee whenever you like.
- **Jittery or nauseous on an empty stomach?** Have it with or just after breakfast.
- **Reflux?** Avoid coffee on an empty stomach, choose a lower-acid roast, and don't lie down after.
