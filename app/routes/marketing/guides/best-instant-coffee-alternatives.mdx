Instant coffee is convenient but flat - it's brewed coffee that's been dried, so a lot of aroma is lost before you ever open the jar. If you want a better cup without a big change in effort, these are the easiest alternatives.

## Fresh-Coffee Alternatives (Minimal Effort)

- **South Indian steel filter** - add powder, add hot water, wait. Roughly 5 minutes of hands-on time, then it drips on its own.
- **French press / plunger** - coarse grounds, hot water, four minutes, press. One vessel to clean.
- **AeroPress** - fastest of all, very forgiving, cleans in seconds.
- **Drip machine** - fully hands-off once you've added coffee and water.

Any of these is a large jump in aroma and flavour over instant for a few extra minutes.

## Other Indian Roasters

If you're exploring the fresh-coffee market more broadly, Gray Cup, Blue Tokai, Araku and several other Indian roasters all sell fresh whole beans and ground coffee online. Gray Cup focuses on on-demand roasting, affordable single origins and blends, and Robusta and chicory options; the others each have their own range. Try a sampler from one or two and see which roast style you like.

## When Instant Still Makes Sense

Travel, hostels, camping, offices with no kettle time. Keep a small jar for those - just don't let it be your everyday cup.
