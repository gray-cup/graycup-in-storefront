Latte, cappuccino, mocha, and plain espresso with milk all start from the same base - a shot (or two) of espresso - and differ mainly in how much milk and foam get added, and whether chocolate is involved. If you've ever stood at a counter unsure what to order, this is the full breakdown.

## The Core Difference: Milk-to-Foam Ratio

| Drink | Espresso | Steamed milk | Milk foam | Chocolate | Typical size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Espresso | 1 shot (~30ml) | none | none | no | 30-60ml |
| Espresso with milk (e.g. cortado) | 1 shot | small, roughly equal to espresso | thin layer | no | 90-120ml |
| Cappuccino | 1 shot | ~equal parts milk and foam | thick, ~1/3 of the cup | no | 150-180ml |
| Caffe Latte | 1-2 shots | mostly milk | thin layer on top | no | 240-350ml |
| Mocha / Cafe Mocha Latte | 1-2 shots | milk | thin foam or whipped cream | yes - chocolate syrup or powder | 240-350ml |
| Flat White | 1-2 shots (ristretto) | steamed milk, very fine microfoam | very thin | no | 150-180ml |

The short version: **cappuccino** is roughly one-third espresso, one-third steamed milk, one-third thick foam - the foam is the point. **Caffe latte** flips that ratio toward milk, with just a thin foam cap, giving a milkier, softer drink. **Mocha** is a latte with chocolate added - sometimes as syrup stirred in, sometimes as cocoa powder dusted or whipped cream on top. **Plain espresso with milk** (a cortado or a simple "espresso and milk") is somewhere between an espresso shot and a cappuccino - just enough milk to soften the shot without turning it into a full milk drink.

## Latte and Cappuccino and Mocha, Side by Side

- **Latte espresso ratio**: mild, milk-forward. Good if you want a large, easy-drinking coffee.
- **Espresso latte cappuccino** confusion usually comes down to foam thickness - if there's a substantial dry foam cap you can spoon, it's a cappuccino; if it's mostly liquid milk with a thin top layer, it's a latte.
- **Cafe latte cappuccino** decision in practice: order a cappuccino if you like a stronger coffee taste per sip and don't mind less milk volume; order a latte if you want a bigger, milkier drink.
- **Cafe latte espresso**: some cafes use this to mean a latte made with a double shot rather than single - ask if you want it stronger.
- **Cappuccino latte espresso** all trace back to the same shot; the espresso itself doesn't change between them, only what's added to it.

## Making These at Home

All of these need a genuinely strong coffee base to hold up under milk - weak coffee just disappears. If you're using a moka pot or French press instead of an espresso machine, lean on a Robusta-forward or 80:20 blend for enough body to come through the milk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a latte stronger than a cappuccino?**
The coffee content is usually the same (one or two shots) but a latte has more milk, so the coffee flavour is diluted more - a cappuccino tastes stronger per sip even with equal espresso.

**What makes a mocha different from a latte?**
Chocolate. A mocha is a latte with chocolate syrup, powder, or sauce added - everything else about the espresso and milk stays the same.

**Is espresso with milk the same as a latte?**
No - "espresso with milk" or a cortado uses much less milk than a latte, closer to a 1:1 ratio, so the coffee flavour is far more prominent.
