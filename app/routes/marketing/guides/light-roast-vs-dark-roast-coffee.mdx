Roast level is the biggest lever on how coffee tastes - bigger than origin for most people's palates. It changes acidity, body, sweetness and bitterness. What it barely changes is caffeine: by weight, dark roast has marginally *less* than light, a difference too small to matter in practice.

## Light Roast

Stopped soon after "first crack." Keeps the most origin character - fruit, floral, tea-like notes - plus the most acidity and a lighter body. Can taste sour or grassy if under-extracted, so it needs a careful brew. Best for pour over and filter, black.

## Medium Roast

The balance point. Acidity softened, sweetness developed (caramelisation), origin still recognisable, body fuller. The most forgiving and the most versatile - black or with a little milk.

## Dark Roast (Vienna, French)

Taken well past second crack. Origin flavour mostly replaced by roast flavour: smoky, bittersweet, chocolatey, low acidity, heavy body, oily surface. Bold and consistent. Best for espresso and strong milk coffee.

## Which Should You Buy?

- **Black coffee, pour over, filter:** light or medium.
- **Espresso, moka pot, strong milk coffee:** medium-dark or dark.
- **Not sure / want forgiving:** medium.
- **Chasing caffeine:** roast doesn't matter - the bean (Robusta) and the dose do.
