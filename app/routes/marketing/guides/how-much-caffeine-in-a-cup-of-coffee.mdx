There's no single number - it depends on three things: the bean, the dose, and the brew. Roast level barely matters.

## The Three Levers

1. **Bean.** Robusta has about **2,400 mg of caffeine per 100 g** of grounds; Arabica about **1,300 mg** - close to double.
2. **Dose.** How many grams of coffee you use. This is the biggest everyday variable. A weak 12 g cup and a strong 30 g cup differ hugely.
3. **Brew.** Longer contact and less dilution extract more. A concentrated decoction or espresso pulls more per gram than a fast, watery drip.

Roast: dark roast has marginally *less* caffeine by weight than light - a difference too small to notice.

A typical home cup uses 12-18 g, not 30, so real-world cups often land around 90-160 mg. Espresso is ~60-100 mg per shot - less total than a big filter coffee, just more concentrated.

## How Much Is Too Much?

About **400 mg a day** is the commonly cited safe ceiling for healthy adults - roughly 3-4 strong cups. Pregnancy guidance is ~200 mg. Past your limit you get jitters, a racing heart and poor sleep, not more energy.

## Want More (or Less)?

- **More:** raise the Robusta share, use more grounds, brew a concentrate.
- **Less:** more Arabica, smaller dose, or a chicory filter blend (chicory has no caffeine).
