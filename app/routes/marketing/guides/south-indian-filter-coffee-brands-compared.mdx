South Indian filter coffee brands span a wide range, from mass-market chicory-forward powders sold in every kirana store to specialty roasters selling single-origin beans online. Instead of comparing them one at a time, it helps to think in two broad groups.

## Mass-Market, Chicory-Forward

These brands prioritise wide availability, consistency and everyday pricing, generally with a thicker decoction achieved through chicory content or a Robusta-leaning ratio:

- **Levista Filter Coffee** - a well-known packaged filter coffee brand with broad retail presence across South India.
- **CCD (Cafe Coffee Day) Filter Coffee** - Cafe Coffee Day's retail filter coffee powder line, extending the cafe brand into packaged coffee for home brewing.
- **Sidapur Coffee** - a filter coffee brand associated with Coorg-region sourcing, positioned toward the traditional filter segment.
- **Bayars Coffee** - a South Indian coffee brand with a retail and cafe presence in parts of the region.

Exact Arabica:Robusta:chicory ratios for these brands aren't consistently published, so take any specific number you see elsewhere with caution - the general pattern across the mass-market segment is a preference for thicker, stronger, chicory-supported decoctions at accessible prices.

## Specialty / Single-Origin-Leaning

A newer wave of Indian coffee brands leans away from the traditional filter format toward specialty-grade, often single-origin beans, sold more for pour-over, French press or espresso than for the steel-filter decoction:

- **Cothas Coffee** - a long-established Bangalore roaster that sells both traditional filter coffee and specialty-leaning options.
- **Sleepy Owl** and **Blue Tokai** - specialty coffee brands built around single-origin sourcing and modern brew methods, generally positioned outside the traditional filter-coffee-with-chicory category entirely.
- Smaller or more niche names like **Aesir** and **Delizio Caffino** also exist in this broader Indian specialty coffee space, though their specific product lines vary and are worth checking directly rather than assuming.

## Where Gray Cup Fits

Gray Cup sits between these two groups rather than squarely in either: it uses the traditional filter coffee format and ratio system (Arabica:Robusta, with chicory as an option) that the mass-market brands are known for, but sources and roasts in small batches the way specialty brands do, with the ratio itself customisable per order.

## Which Group Fits You

If you want the classic filter-coffee-with-milk ritual at an everyday price, the mass-market chicory-forward brands are built for that. If you're brewing pour-over or espresso and want single-origin character, the specialty brands are the better fit. If you want the traditional filter format and ratio but with specialty-grade sourcing and a ratio you control, that's the gap Gray Cup fills.
