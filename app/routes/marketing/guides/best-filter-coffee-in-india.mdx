South Indian filter coffee is a method as much as a product: finely ground coffee packed into a two-part steel filter, hot water dripped slowly through it to make a thick decoction, then mixed with hot milk and sugar and pulled between two tumblers for froth. The cup you get depends almost entirely on the powder - the Arabica/Robusta ratio, the roast, and whether there's chicory.

## Choosing Your Blend

- **More Arabica** - smoother, more aroma, lighter body.
- **More Robusta** - stronger, more bitter, more crema-like body, more caffeine.
- **With chicory** - smoother, mellower, thicker decoction, less caffeine, classic Kumbakonam-style flavour.

The traditional ratio is 70:30 (Arabica:Robusta), or 80:20 with 20-30% chicory for the "degree coffee" taste.

## How to Brew a Good Decoction

1. Use 2-3 tablespoons of powder per cup, packed firm but not compressed.
2. Pour water just off the boil, fill the top chamber, close the lid.
3. Wait 15-20 minutes for it to drip fully. Don't rush it.
4. Mix roughly 1 part decoction to 3-4 parts hot milk, sweeten, and froth by pouring between tumblers.
