Honey processed coffee has nothing to do with honey. The name comes from the sticky, tacky feel of the beans as they dry with some of the fruit's sugary mucilage still attached. It's also called "pulped natural."

## How It's Made

1. The cherry skin is removed (like washed coffee).
2. But the mucilage - the sweet, sticky layer under the skin - is left on, partly or fully.
3. The beans dry in the sun with that layer intact, for one to three weeks.
4. The dried mucilage is then removed and the beans are milled.

How much mucilage is left, and how it's dried, gives "white," "yellow," "red" and "black" honey - more mucilage and slower drying means more fruit character and more risk.

## How It Tastes

Honey coffee sits between washed and natural:

- **Sweeter** than washed - caramel, brown sugar, stone fruit.
- **Rounder, syrupy body**, more than washed, less wild than natural.
- **Lower, softer acidity** than a washed coffee.
- **Cleaner** than a full natural - less boozy or funky.

It's a good pick if washed coffees taste thin to you but naturals taste too much.
