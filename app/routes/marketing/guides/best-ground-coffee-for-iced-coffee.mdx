Pour a normal cup of hot coffee over ice and you'll mostly taste watered-down coffee - the ice melts fast in a hot liquid and dilutes it before you've even taken a sip. The fix isn't a special bag of "iced coffee" beans, it's brewing stronger than usual and being deliberate about how the ice meets the coffee.

## Iced Coffee vs Cold Brew - They're Not the Same Thing

People use "iced coffee" and "cold brew" interchangeably, but they're different drinks:

- **Iced coffee** is brewed hot (drip, filter, French press, whatever you normally use) and then either chilled or poured straight over ice. It's ready in minutes.
- **Cold brew** is ground coffee steeped in room-temperature or cold water for 12-24 hours, then strained. No heat at all, and a naturally smoother, less acidic result.

Both are valid, but this recipe is for iced coffee - hot-brewed, served cold - because it's faster and doesn't need a day of planning. If you want the smoother, low-acid style, that's a different process worth trying separately; for now, see [Black Coffee Benefits](/guides/black-coffee-benefits) for why either version, taken without milk, is worth getting used to.

## What Coffee to Use

Any of your usual ground coffee works, but the ratio matters more than the specific blend:

- **A medium-to-dark roast** holds up better over ice than a very light roast, which can taste thin once diluted.
- **A stronger grind ratio** - roughly double the coffee-to-water you'd use for a normal hot cup - is the single biggest lever. If you usually use 2 tablespoons per cup, use 4.
- **A moka pot or French press concentrate** works well too, since both naturally produce a stronger, more concentrated brew than a drip filter.

## Simple Iced Coffee Recipe (No Milk)

1. Brew your coffee at roughly double strength - about 4 tablespoons of ground coffee for every 200ml of water, using your usual method (filter, French press, or moka pot).
2. Fill a tall glass with ice - more ice than you think you need, since some of it will melt into the drink.
3. Pour the hot, double-strength coffee directly over the ice. It cools and dilutes to a normal strength as it hits the ice.
4. Stir well and taste. If it's still too strong, add a splash of cold water; if it's weak, you didn't brew strong enough - note it for next time.
5. Optional: a few drops of sugar syrup (sugar dissolves poorly in cold liquid, so pre-dissolve it in a little hot water first) if you want it sweetened.

That's it - no milk, no cream, just concentrated coffee properly diluted by ice instead of accidentally diluted by it.

## A Quick Note on Consistency

Because the ice is doing double duty (chilling and diluting), the amount of ice you use changes the final strength every time. If you want a repeatable cup, measure your ice by the glass rather than eyeballing it, and keep your coffee-to-water ratio consistent.
