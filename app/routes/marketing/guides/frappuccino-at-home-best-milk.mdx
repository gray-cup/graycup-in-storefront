A frappuccino gets confused with iced lattes and milkshakes a lot, but it's really its own thing. It's not brewed hot and poured over ice like an iced latte, and it's not built on ice cream like a milkshake - it's strong cold coffee, ice, milk, and usually a sweetener, all blended together into a thick, slushy, drinkable-through-a-straw texture. The blending is what makes it a frappuccino; skip the blender and you just have iced coffee with milk in it.

## Best Milk for Frappuccino: Comparing Your Options

The milk you choose changes the texture more than the flavour, since blending with ice mutes subtle taste differences anyway.

- **Whole milk** - the creamiest, richest result. The fat helps it blend into a smoother, less icy slush and holds its texture longer before it starts to separate.
- **2% or skim milk** - blends fine but comes out icier and thinner, closer to a coffee slushie than a creamy one. Good if you want something lighter.
- **Oat milk** - blends and froths well, and its natural body helps it hold up to the ice better than most other plant milks, so it's a solid dairy-free pick.
- **Almond milk** - works, but it's thinner and can turn watery once the ice melts a little, so the drink loses body faster than with whole milk or oat milk.

If you want the closest thing to a café-style frappuccino, whole milk or oat milk are the two to reach for first.

## Basic No-Machine Recipe

You don't need a fancy machine - a regular blender does the job.

1. Brew a strong batch of coffee (double-strength or espresso-style) and chill it, or use it straight from the fridge if you keep cold-brew on hand.
2. Add roughly 1 cup ice, ½ cup milk of choice, ½ cup chilled coffee, and 1-2 tablespoons sweetener (sugar, simple syrup, or a flavoured syrup) to the blender.
3. Blend on high for 20-30 seconds until it's a uniform slushy texture with no ice chunks.
4. Taste and adjust - more ice for thicker, more coffee for stronger, more milk for creamier.
