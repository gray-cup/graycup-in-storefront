Nescafe is, for most people, synonymous with instant coffee - the granules-and-hot-water format that's been a household staple in India for decades. Nescafe also sells a filter coffee product, but it's worth understanding where that sits before comparing it to a dedicated filter coffee blend like Gray Cup's.

## Where Nescafe's Filter Coffee Sits

Nescafe's filter coffee line exists at the crossover between instant coffee and traditional South Indian filter coffee - a packaged powder aimed at people who want something closer to the filter coffee taste without necessarily changing their usual routine, from a brand whose manufacturing scale and expertise are built around instant coffee first. That crossover positioning is a reasonable middle ground for convenience, but it's a different starting point from a brand built entirely around the decoction method.

## Where Gray Cup Sits

Gray Cup's filter coffee isn't a line extension from an instant-coffee catalogue - it's the whole product, brewed the traditional way: fine grounds packed into a steel filter, dripped slowly into a thick decoction, then mixed with hot milk.

- **Method-first** - built specifically for decoction brewing, not adapted from an instant format.
- **Ratio choice** - pick your Arabica:Robusta balance, or add chicory for a thicker, mellower cup.
- **Roasted to order** - ground close to when you order rather than sitting in long retail distribution.

## Which One Fits You

If your priority is speed and you're comfortable with a packaged, instant-adjacent product from a familiar brand, Nescafe's filter coffee line is a reasonable convenience option. If you want the traditional decoction experience - and the ability to choose your own ratio and get a fresher roast - a dedicated filter coffee blend like Gray Cup's is built for that specifically.
