Bulletproof coffee is black coffee blended (not just stirred) with a fat - traditionally grass-fed butter or ghee - and a source of MCT (medium-chain triglyceride) oil or coconut oil, until it turns frothy and almost latte-like. It was popularised as a breakfast replacement, especially by people doing intermittent fasting or a ketogenic-style diet, since it adds fat and satiety without the carbs of a regular breakfast.

## Why the Base Coffee Matters More Here

In a regular cup, milk and sugar cover up a lot of flaws in the coffee. In bulletproof coffee, butter and oil don't mask bitterness the same way - they add richness, but a harsh, over-extracted, or stale coffee still tastes harsh underneath the fat. Because of that, you want a base coffee that's:

- **Strong** - brewed a bit more concentrated than usual, since it's about to be diluted in flavour (if not volume) by the fat.
- **Clean and low-bitterness** - a good medium-to-dark roast without a burnt or ashy edge holds up much better blended than a very light, sharp roast.
- **Fresh** - stale coffee's flat, cardboard-y notes get more noticeable once you're drinking it slowly, without milk to hide it.

## A Simple Recipe

1. Brew roughly 1.5-2 cups of strong black coffee (French press or filter both work well).
2. Add 1-2 tablespoons of unsalted grass-fed butter or ghee.
3. Add 1 teaspoon to 1 tablespoon of MCT oil or coconut oil - start low if you're new to it, since MCT oil can cause stomach upset in larger amounts.
4. Blend for 20-30 seconds in a regular blender until frothy and emulsified - a spoon or whisk won't fully combine the fat, you'll just get an oil slick on top.

Pour hot and drink it fresh; it separates again if it sits too long.

## Is It Right for You?

Bulletproof coffee is calorie-dense - a tablespoon each of butter and MCT oil adds roughly 200-250 calories, mostly saturated and MCT fat. That can be useful if your goal is appetite control during a fast or extra fuel on a low-carb plan, but it isn't automatically a "healthy" addition for everyone, and it doesn't suit every diet goal. This isn't medical or nutrition advice - if you're using it as part of an intermittent fasting or ketogenic protocol, it's worth checking the approach with a doctor or nutritionist first.
