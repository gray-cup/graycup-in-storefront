Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) - now often called MASLD - is one of the most common liver conditions worldwide, and diet plays a big role in managing it. Coffee is one of the few beverages that shows up consistently in this research: several studies have linked regular coffee drinking to a lower risk of developing fatty liver, less severe fibrosis in people who already have it, and better liver enzyme readings overall. The effect seems strongest with black coffee, since the added milk, cream and sugar in most coffee drinks work against the same metabolic goals coffee is being credited for.

This isn't a substitute for medical advice or treatment - fatty liver needs to be managed with a doctor, especially around weight, alcohol and sugar intake. But if you're already looking to add black coffee to your routine, the beans you actually enjoy drinking black make the difference between a habit that sticks and one that doesn't.

Here's what to look for in a black coffee brand, and five picks worth trying.

## What Makes a Coffee "Good" for Fatty Liver

There's no single fatty-liver coffee brand backed by clinical trials - the research is about coffee as a category, particularly filtered, brewed black coffee drunk consistently over time. A few things matter more than the label:

- **Ground, not instant.** Instant coffee goes through more processing, which reduces the chlorogenic acid and other plant polyphenols thought to be behind coffee's liver benefits. Ground, freshly brewed coffee retains more of them.
- **No added sugar or cream.** Fatty liver management is closely tied to reducing added sugar and saturated fat - pouring both into your coffee undoes a lot of the point.
- **Something you'll drink every day.** The studies showing benefit are about habitual intake over months and years, not one cup on a good day. A coffee that's too bitter or harsh to drink black consistently isn't the right choice, no matter what's in it.

## How Much Coffee, and How to Brew It

Most of the studies showing a benefit for fatty liver looked at people drinking around 2 to 3 cups of coffee a day - more wasn't shown to add further benefit, and too much late in the day will just cost you sleep. Filter or pour-over both work fine; there's no strong evidence that brew method changes the benefit, only that ground, brewed coffee outperforms instant.

## A Few Practical Notes

- **Skip the sugar, skip the cream.** If fatty liver is the reason you're drinking black coffee, adding sugar and saturated fat back in works against the point.
- **Consistency matters more than the specific bean.** Any of the coffees above work - the research is about habitual intake, not a single miracle brand.
- **This supports a plan, it isn't the plan.** Weight management, cutting added sugar and alcohol, and regular checkups with a doctor are what actually treat fatty liver. Coffee is a small, low-cost addition on top of that.

---

If you already drink coffee, taking it black - and picking beans you genuinely enjoy that way - is one of the easiest dietary habits to keep. None of this is medical advice, so talk to a doctor about managing fatty liver directly, but there's no downside to making your daily coffee a black one.
