Espresso *is* black coffee - there's no milk in it. The real comparison is espresso versus a regular brewed black coffee (filter, pour over, press). The difference is how it's made: espresso forces hot water through finely ground coffee under about 9 bar of pressure, producing a small, concentrated, syrupy shot with crema. Brewed black coffee drips or steeps at normal pressure into a larger, lighter cup.

## Side by Side

| | Brewed black coffee | Espresso |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | 200-350 ml | 25-40 ml |
| Caffeine per serving | ~90-200 mg (bigger cup) | ~60-100 mg per shot |
| Caffeine per ml | Low | Very high |
| Strength / body | Lighter, cleaner | Intense, syrupy |
| Crema | None | Yes |
| Kit | Kettle + dripper/press | Pump machine + grinder |
| Best beans | Any, esp. light/medium single origin | Fresh medium-dark, often with Robusta |

## The Caffeine Myth

A single espresso has *less* total caffeine than a mug of filter coffee, because the mug is far bigger. Espresso only "feels" stronger because it's concentrated. If you want the biggest caffeine hit, a large strong filter coffee beats one shot.

## Which Should You Drink?

Espresso if you want a quick, intense shot or a base for milk drinks and own a machine. Brewed black coffee if you want a longer, more sippable cup, more total caffeine, and simpler kit.
