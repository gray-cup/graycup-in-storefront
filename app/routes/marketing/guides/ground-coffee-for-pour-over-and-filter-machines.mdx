"Ground coffee for filter," "ground coffee for V60," "ground coffee for stovetop espresso" - these all sound like the same question, but they're really three different grind sizes for three different brewers. Grouping them under one "filter coffee" label is where most buying mistakes happen: a grind that's perfect for an electric drip machine will run too fast and thin through a V60, and a grind fine enough for a stovetop moka pot will clog a paper filter completely. Here's a quick reference for each.

## Pour-Over (V60 and Similar Drippers)

Pour-over drippers - the Hario V60 is the best-known example, a cone-shaped dripper with a spiral ridge inside - rely on you controlling the water pour by hand, which means the grind needs to be even and medium in size, similar to granulated sugar. Water passes through once, under gravity rather than pressure, so a grind that's too fine will choke the flow and over-extract, while too coarse will run through fast and taste weak and sour.

If you're shopping for "ground coffee for V60" specifically (rather than looking to buy a V60 itself - that's a dripper, not something we sell), ask for a medium, pour-over-specific grind, and expect to adjust pour speed and grind together as you dial it in.

## Stovetop Moka Pot ("Stovetop Espresso")

What people often mean by "ground coffee for stovetop espresso" is a moka pot - the octagonal stovetop pot that pushes steam-pressurised water up through a puck of grounds. It's not true espresso (much lower pressure), but it wants a grind in a similar direction: fine, though not quite as fine as a pump espresso machine needs. Grind too fine here and, like with a V60, you'll choke the pot and risk it stalling or even becoming unsafe to leave unattended; too coarse and you'll get a thin, watery result instead of the concentrated cup a moka pot is meant to produce.

## Electric Drip / Filter Machines

For an ordinary electric drip filter machine - the kind with a basket, a paper or reusable filter, and an automatic hot water drip - a medium grind, similar to what you'd use for pour-over, is the standard choice. Most bags labelled simply "ground coffee for filter machines" or "best ground coffee for filter" are cut to this medium spec by default, which is why it's the safest generic choice if you don't yet know which method you're buying for.

## Quick Reference

| Method | Grind size |
|---|---|
| Pour-over (V60 etc.) | Medium |
| Stovetop moka pot | Fine (not espresso-fine) |
| Electric drip / filter machine | Medium |
