The moka pot (stovetop "Bialetti") forces steam-pressurised water up through a bed of coffee. It's not true espresso - the pressure is much lower - but it makes a strong, concentrated, near-espresso cup. It suits bold, low-acid roasts, and a grind between filter and espresso.

## What Works in a Moka Pot

- **Medium-dark to dark roast** - the concentration amplifies acidity, so bright light roasts can taste sour and sharp.
- **Fine-ish grind** - finer than filter, coarser than espresso. Too fine clogs and builds dangerous pressure.
- **Fill the basket level, don't tamp.** Moka pots want loose grounds.
- **Take it off the heat** as soon as it splutters, and cool the base under a tap to stop extraction.

## Quick Method

1. Fill the base with hot water to just below the valve.
2. Level the basket with coffee, no tamping.
3. Assemble, medium heat, lid open.
4. When it gurgles and lightens in colour, pull it off and cool the base.
