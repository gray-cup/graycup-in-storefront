Search "best ground coffee for espresso machine" and you'll find dozens of pages recommending specific brands as if the machine cared. It doesn't. Every espresso machine - DeLonghi, Breville, a Dedica, a bar-grade commercial unit - runs hot water at pressure through a puck of ground coffee. What makes that puck work has nothing to do with the badge on the bag and everything to do with grind size, dose, and how fresh the roast is.

## Why Grind Size Matters More Than Brand

Espresso is brewed fast - 25 to 30 seconds - and under 9 bars of pressure. To extract properly in that short window, the coffee has to be ground very fine, closer to table salt or fine sand than to the coarser grind you'd use for drip or French press. Too coarse and water rushes through, giving you a thin, sour, under-extracted shot. Too fine and it chokes, extracting bitter and over. This is why "espresso ground coffee for espresso machine" isn't a marketing phrase, it's a real grind spec: fine, even, and consistent from particle to particle.

If you're buying pre-ground coffee for espresso, ask (or look) for a fine, espresso-specific grind rather than a generic "ground coffee" bag, which is usually cut for a drip machine and will under-extract badly in an espresso basket.

## The Freshness Problem With Pre-Ground Espresso Coffee

Here's the part most buying guides skip: fine grinds stale faster than any other grind size. Grinding coffee multiplies its surface area, and a fine espresso grind exposes far more of that surface to air than a coarse French press grind does. Aromatic oils and CO2 that carry flavour escape within days, sometimes hours, of grinding. That's why a bag of pre ground coffee for espresso that's been sitting on a shelf for weeks - however well-known the name on it - often pulls flat, muted shots no matter how well you dial in your machine.

The fix isn't a specific brand. It's buying the smallest amount you'll use within two to three weeks, checking the roast date (not just a "best before" date, which tells you almost nothing), and buying from a roaster that grinds close to when it ships rather than warehousing pre-ground stock.

## Does Your Machine Brand Actually Need Special Coffee?

No. This applies whether you're using ground coffee for a DeLonghi espresso machine, a Dedica, or any other pressurised brewer. Machine manufacturers don't formulate proprietary grinds - a DeLonghi machine, a Breville, or a commercial group head all pull espresso the same physical way, and any properly fine, fresh ground coffee will work in it. Brands like Lavazza or Costa sell ground coffee marketed for espresso, and it's worth knowing what that means in practice: it's espresso-fine, roasted for a bolder cup, and nothing more machine-specific than that. There's no lock-in - any roaster's fine espresso grind is "ground coffee suitable for espresso machine" as long as the grind size and freshness are right.

If you want decaf ground coffee for an espresso machine, the same rules apply unchanged: fine and even grind, fresh roast, correct dose. Decaffeination doesn't change the physics of extraction.

## What to Actually Buy

- **Grind:** fine, even, espresso-specific - ask your roaster to grind for espresso, not "medium" or "filter."
- **Roast:** within 2-4 weeks of the roast date, ideally closer to fresh.
- **Dose:** roughly 18-20g for a double shot as a starting point, adjusted to your basket size.
- **Blend:** a Robusta-inclusive blend gives more crema and body; a higher-Arabica blend gives more aroma and acidity. Both work in any espresso machine.
