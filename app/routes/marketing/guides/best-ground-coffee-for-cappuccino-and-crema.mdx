There's no single "best brand" for crema - it's a byproduct of four things working together: a fresh roast, a fine and even grind, some Robusta in the blend, and pressure forcing water through the puck. Get those four right with an ordinary moka pot or basic home espresso machine and you'll see a reasonable crema layer without any professional gear.

## What Actually Makes Crema

- **Freshness** - crema is mostly trapped CO2 and oils released during roasting. Coffee more than a few weeks off-roast has lost most of that gas, so it foams less no matter how good the machine is.
- **Grind size** - crema needs resistance. Too coarse and water rushes through with no pressure buildup; too fine and it chokes the machine. For espresso you want a fine, sand-like, even grind - burr-ground, not blade-ground, since blade grinders produce uneven particles that brew inconsistently.
- **Robusta content** - Robusta beans have roughly double the natural oils and different solubility than Arabica, which produces a thicker, more persistent crema. Pure Arabica coffee can still taste great but tends to give a thinner, faster-fading crema.
- **Pressure** - a moka pot generates a few bars of pressure, enough for a decent crema-like foam; a real espresso machine at 9 bars gives you more of it. Neither works without the first three.

## A Crema-Friendly Blend

For cappuccino and espresso-style drinks at home, a blend with meaningful Robusta content gives you the best shot at visible crema without needing commercial-grade equipment.

If you already have a bag of pure Arabica and don't want to switch, you can still get a cappuccino out of it - the crema will just be lighter and disappear faster, which is a cosmetic difference more than a flavour one.

## Getting a Better Cappuccino at Home

1. **Buy smaller amounts, more often.** Freshness matters more than any other single factor - a bag that's been open for six weeks won't crema well.
2. **Grind fine and even, right before brewing.** If you don't have a burr grinder, ask for your coffee ground fine for espresso/moka use at the time of purchase and use it up quickly.
3. **Pack it evenly.** In a moka pot, fill the basket level and don't compress it hard; in a portafilter, tamp firmly and evenly so water doesn't find a weak spot to rush through.
4. **Steam or froth your milk separately.** Cappuccino's texture comes as much from the milk as the espresso - froth it to a fine, glossy microfoam rather than large bubbles, then pour over your shot.
5. **Don't judge crema alone.** A pale or thin crema with good flavour underneath is still a good cup - crema is a nice-to-have indicator of freshness, not the point of the drink.
