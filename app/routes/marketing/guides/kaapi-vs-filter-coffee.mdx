If you've searched "kaapi vs filter coffee" hoping to find two different drinks, the short answer is: they're the same thing. "Kaapi" is simply the Kannada and Tamil word for coffee, used colloquially to mean the whole South Indian filter coffee ritual - decoction, hot milk, sugar, poured between a tumbler and davara for froth. "Filter kaapi" and "filter coffee" describe the identical drink; the word choice is regional, not a different recipe.

## Same Drink, Different Name

South Indian filter coffee is made with a two-part steel filter: coarsely-to-finely ground coffee packed into the upper chamber, hot water dripped through slowly to make a thick decoction, then mixed with hot milk and sugar. Whether you call the result "filter coffee" or "kaapi" depends mostly on where you grew up and which language you default to - not on the brewing method.

**Hatti Kaapi**, a well-known Karnataka-based coffee brand, uses "Kaapi" directly in its name and is one of the more recognisable examples of the term being used as a product brand rather than just a colloquial word.

## Regional Style Notes

Even though it's one drink, the exact style does shift by region:

- **Chennai style** - often described as "degree coffee," typically leaning chicory-forward for a thick, mellow, slightly sweet decoction that suits the city's cafe culture.
- **Kumbakonam style** - traditionally one of the more chicory-heavy, strong versions, associated with a particularly thick decoction and a devoted local following.
- **Karnataka "kaapi"** - often made with less chicory than the Tamil Nadu styles, leaning closer to a straight Arabica-Robusta filter coffee.

None of these are strict rules - individual households and cafes vary - but they're the general regional tendencies people are usually referring to when they distinguish "Chennai filter coffee" from "Kumbakonam filter coffee near me" searches.
