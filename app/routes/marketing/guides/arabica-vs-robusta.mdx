Nearly all coffee is one of two species: *Coffea arabica* or *Coffea canephora* (Robusta). India grows both. Neither is simply "better" - they're built for different jobs.

## The Differences

| | Arabica | Robusta |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine | ~1,300 mg / 100 g | ~2,400 mg / 100 g |
| Flavour | Sweeter, fruity, floral, more acidity | Harsher, woody, nutty, grainy |
| Bitterness | Lower | Higher |
| Body | Lighter to medium | Heavy, full |
| Crema (espresso) | Thin | Thick |
| Growing | High altitude, fussy, disease-prone | Lower altitude, hardy, higher yield |
| Price | Higher | Lower |

## When to Choose Arabica

- You drink coffee **black** and want aroma and nuance.
- **Pour over, French press, drip** - brew methods that show off origin character.
- You find Robusta harsh.

## When to Choose Robusta (or a Blend)

- You take coffee **with milk and sugar** and want it to cut through.
- **South Indian filter or espresso** - Robusta adds body, crema and a stronger kick.
- You want **more caffeine** for less money.

## The Honest Answer

For black coffee and filter/pour-over at home, Arabica or a high-Arabica blend usually wins. For milk coffee, espresso and maximum caffeine per rupee, some Robusta earns its place. Most great filter and espresso blends use both.
