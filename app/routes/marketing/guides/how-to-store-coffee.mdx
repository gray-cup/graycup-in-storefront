Roasted coffee doesn't spoil in a way that's unsafe, but it goes stale - flat, cardboard-like, lifeless - and that happens faster than most people think. Four things do the damage: oxygen, heat, light and moisture.

## The Rules

- **Airtight.** Oxygen is the main enemy. An opaque, well-sealed container or the original valve bag rolled down tight.
- **Cool and dark.** A cupboard, not the counter next to the stove or a sunny shelf.
- **Dry.** Never store coffee near the kettle steam or in the fridge door (humidity + odours).
- **Whole beans over ground.** Ground coffee stales in days; beans hold for weeks.
- **Buy to your usage.** 250-500 g at a time. A kilo bag is stale before an average household finishes it.

## Timeline

- **Whole beans:** best 5-21 days off roast; good for ~4-6 weeks sealed.
- **Ground coffee:** best within 1-2 weeks; noticeably flat after 3-4.
- **Opened bag:** use within 2-3 weeks, kept airtight.

## The Freezer Question

Freezing works *if* done right: freeze in airtight, single-use portions, and take a portion out and let it come fully to room temperature before opening (condensation on cold beans is what ruins them). Don't repeatedly open and refreeze one bag. For most people, just buying smaller and fresher is simpler.
