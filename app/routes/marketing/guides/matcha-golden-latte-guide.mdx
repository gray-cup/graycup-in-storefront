Not every drink called a "latte" involves coffee at all. Matcha lattes and golden (turmeric) lattes both borrow the word for the same reason a caffe latte earns it - steamed or warmed milk is the base - but neither one contains espresso or coffee. If you've searched for a matcha latte or a golden latte at Starbucks and wondered how they compare to an actual coffee latte, here's the honest breakdown.

## Matcha Latte: What It Actually Is

A **matcha green tea latte** is finely ground green tea powder (matcha) whisked into a small amount of hot water to form a smooth paste, then combined with steamed milk - the same steamed-milk technique as a coffee latte, just built on tea instead of espresso. Matcha has an earthy, slightly grassy, umami flavour that's quite different from black tea, and it's traditionally whisked rather than stirred to avoid clumping.

**Does matcha have caffeine?** Yes - matcha comes from the same tea plant as green and black tea, so it does contain caffeine, typically less per serving than a shot of espresso but more than most other teas, since you're consuming the whole ground leaf rather than a steeped-and-discarded one. So a matcha latte is not a coffee drink, but it isn't caffeine-free either - it's a genuinely different, moderate caffeine source from tea leaf rather than coffee bean.

## Golden (Turmeric) Latte: What It Actually Is

A **golden latte** or **turmeric latte** is warm milk blended or whisked with turmeric, plus usually ginger, a pinch of black pepper (which helps the body absorb turmeric's curcumin), cinnamon, and a sweetener like honey. There's no tea and no coffee in a traditional golden latte, which makes it genuinely **caffeine-free** - a meaningful difference from both a coffee latte and a matcha latte, and the main reason people reach for it in the evening or as a coffee alternative.

A **spice latte** in the same general family usually refers to a warm-milk drink built around cinnamon, cardamom, or chai-style spices rather than turmeric specifically - same steamed-milk method, different spice blend.

## Matcha vs Golden Latte vs an Actual Coffee Latte

| | Base | Caffeine |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee latte | Espresso + steamed milk | Highest (coffee bean) |
| Matcha latte | Matcha powder + steamed milk | Moderate (tea leaf) |
| Golden/turmeric latte | Turmeric, ginger, pepper + warm milk | None |

If you're choosing between these because of caffeine sensitivity, timing (afternoon vs evening), or simply flavour preference, that table is really the whole decision - a real coffee latte for the strongest lift, matcha for a gentler, tea-based one, and golden milk when you want the ritual of a warm milk drink with none of the caffeine. For a closer look at how caffeine actually varies across coffee itself, see [How Much Caffeine Is in a Cup of Coffee?](/guides/how-much-caffeine-in-a-cup-of-coffee).

## Simple Matcha Latte at Home

1. Sift 1/2-1 teaspoon of matcha powder into a cup to break up clumps.
2. Add 2-3 tablespoons of hot (not boiling) water and whisk briskly until smooth and slightly frothy.
3. Steam or froth 150-200ml of milk and pour it in.
4. Sweeten to taste - honey or a light syrup both work.

## Simple Golden (Turmeric) Latte at Home

1. Warm 200ml of milk gently on the stove (don't boil it).
2. Whisk in 1/2 teaspoon turmeric, a small pinch of black pepper, a pinch of ground ginger, and a pinch of cinnamon.
3. Sweeten with honey or sugar, whisk until frothy, and pour.

## If You Actually Want the Coffee Version

If what you're really after is a milk-forward drink with real coffee flavour and a proper caffeine kick, a classic caffe latte made from a good filter coffee blend gets you there without needing matcha or turmeric at all.
