Caffeine works by blocking adenosine, the molecule that builds sleep pressure through the day. That's great at 10 am and a problem at 10 pm - because caffeine you drank in the afternoon is often still in your system.

## The Half-Life Problem

Caffeine's half-life is roughly 5-6 hours in a healthy adult (longer if you're pregnant, on certain medications, or a slow metaboliser). So if you have 200 mg at 4 pm:

- ~100 mg is still active at 9-10 pm
- ~50 mg at 3-4 am

Even if you fall asleep fine, that residual caffeine reduces deep sleep and total sleep time - studies show measurable disruption from caffeine taken 6 hours before bed.

## Practical Rules

- **Last cup by early afternoon** - around 2 pm if you're sensitive, 3-4 pm for most people, earlier if you sleep before 10.
- **Count all sources** - tea, cola, energy drinks, dark chocolate, pre-workout, some painkillers.
- **Watch the total**, not just timing - a big morning dose can still leave enough at night.
- **If you sleep badly, cut afternoon coffee first** before blaming anything else.
