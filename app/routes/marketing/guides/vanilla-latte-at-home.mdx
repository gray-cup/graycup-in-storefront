"Vanilla latte," "vanilla bean latte," and "French vanilla latte" sound like three names for the same drink, and menus rarely explain the difference. They're related but not identical - the difference is mostly about what kind of vanilla is used and whether it's a real ingredient or just a flavour profile.

## Vanilla Latte vs Vanilla Bean Latte vs French Vanilla

- **Vanilla latte** - espresso and steamed milk sweetened with vanilla syrup. The syrup is usually a clear or lightly coloured sugar syrup flavoured with vanilla extract or artificial vanillin. Clean, simple, consistent.
- **Vanilla bean latte** - made with vanilla bean paste or scraped vanilla seeds instead of syrup. You'll often see actual black specks in the drink. The flavour is rounder and less purely sweet than syrup, closer to real vanilla pod than "vanilla flavouring."
- **French vanilla latte** - despite the name, this has nothing to do with France or with a different vanilla plant. "French vanilla" is a flavour profile - a richer, more custard-like vanilla note (originally associated with French vanilla ice cream, which uses an egg-custard base) - built with a syrup formulated to taste creamier and slightly eggy-sweet rather than sharply sweet. An iced version of the same syrup is a French vanilla iced latte; nothing else changes.

So: syrup vs real vanilla bean is the "vanilla" vs "vanilla bean" distinction, and "French" describes a flavour style, not an origin or a different drink category.

## Simple Vanilla Latte at Home

**You'll need:** strong coffee (espresso, moka pot, or a strong filter coffee decoction), milk, vanilla extract or syrup, sweetener to taste.

1. Brew about 60-90ml of strong coffee.
2. Stir in 1-2 teaspoons of vanilla syrup, or 1/4 teaspoon of pure vanilla extract plus a teaspoon of sugar, while the coffee is still hot so it dissolves evenly.
3. Steam or froth about 150-200ml of milk (a frother, a shaken jar microwaved briefly, or a French press plunger pumped in hot milk all work).
4. Pour the milk over the coffee, holding back the foam, then spoon the foam on top.

For a **vanilla bean latte** at home, swap the syrup for a small scrape of vanilla bean paste (widely available in baking sections) stirred into the hot coffee - you'll get visible specks and a rounder flavour than syrup gives.

For a **vanilla ice latte**, just build the same drink cold: pour the vanilla-sweetened coffee over ice, then top with cold milk instead of steaming it.

A pure-Arabica or 80:20 Arabica-heavy blend works best under vanilla - it's smoother and less bitter, so the vanilla doesn't have to fight the coffee for attention the way it would with a heavier Robusta cup.
