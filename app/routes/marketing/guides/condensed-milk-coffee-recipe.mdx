Espresso and sweetened condensed milk is one of the simplest coffee upgrades there is: one ingredient does the job of milk and sugar at once, and it's nearly impossible to get wrong. Vietnamese cà phê sữa and the Spanish cortado condensado both lean on the same trick - strong coffee poured onto (or whisked with) condensed milk instead of regular milk plus a spoon of sugar.

## Why Condensed Milk Works So Well With Strong Coffee

- **It's pre-sweetened and pre-thickened.** Regular milk needs added sugar to balance strong, bitter coffee; condensed milk already has both the sugar and a thick, syrupy body baked in, so there's no separate sweetening step.
- **It holds up against strong coffee.** Because it's concentrated, a small amount of condensed milk can balance a genuinely strong shot without diluting it the way a larger volume of regular milk would.
- **It doesn't need heat to combine well.** Unlike regular milk, which is nicer steamed, condensed milk mixes into both hot and cold coffee just by stirring or whisking.

## The Ratio

Start with roughly **1-2 tablespoons of condensed milk per shot of espresso** (or an equivalent small cup of strong coffee), and adjust from there - condensed milk brands vary in sweetness, so treat this as a starting point rather than a fixed rule.

## Hot Recipe

1. Spoon 1-2 tablespoons of sweetened condensed milk into the bottom of a small cup.
2. Brew a shot of espresso or a small cup of strong coffee directly on top of it.
3. Stir well until the condensed milk fully dissolves into the coffee. It should look pale tan and taste rich, not just sweet.

## Iced Recipe

1. Add 1-2 tablespoons of condensed milk to a tall glass.
2. Brew a double-strength shot or small batch of strong coffee (see our [iced coffee guide](/guides/best-ground-coffee-for-iced-coffee) for brewing double strength) and pour it in while hot, stirring to dissolve the condensed milk fully.
3. Fill the rest of the glass with ice. The coffee cools and dilutes slightly as it sits on the ice, which balances the sweetness.

## A Frothier Version

For a lighter texture, whisk the condensed milk and hot coffee together vigorously (by hand with a small whisk, or with a handheld milk frother) for 15-20 seconds before pouring over ice or serving hot. This introduces a bit of air and gives the coffee a light, almost mousse-like top layer - a shortcut some Vietnamese and Thai iced coffee recipes use in place of a shaker.

A stronger, more robust coffee holds its own better against the sweetness of condensed milk than a delicate light roast does, which is why darker or Robusta-leaning blends are the more common choice for this style.
