Caffeine is one of the few things shown, repeatedly, to sharpen attention, reaction time, alertness and short-term recall. That's why it's the default study and work drug worldwide. For focus specifically you want a steady, moderate dose - not a single huge cup that spikes you and then leaves you jittery and unable to sit still.

## What Works for Focus

- **Moderate dose, repeated.** ~1-2 mg caffeine per kg bodyweight per serving, one cup every 3-4 hours. Small and frequent beats one big hit.
- **Black, or with minimal milk.** Sugar spikes then crashes your energy - the opposite of what you want mid-study.
- **Stop ~6 hours before bed.** Poor sleep destroys next-day focus, so an evening study cup borrows from tomorrow.
- **Pair with water.** Mild dehydration reads as brain fog.

## A Note for Students

Caffeine helps you *use* what you've studied - it doesn't replace sleep or spaced revision. Pulling an all-nighter on coffee reliably scores worse than sleeping and reviewing in the morning. Use coffee to stay sharp during the day, not to skip rest.
