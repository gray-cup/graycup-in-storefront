Black coffee - just coffee and water, no milk or sugar - has a genuinely good evidence base for a few things and a lot of hype for others. Here's the honest split. None of this is medical advice.

## Well-Supported Benefits

- **Alertness and focus.** Caffeine blocks adenosine, improving reaction time, vigilance and short-term recall. Reliable and immediate.
- **Exercise performance.** A pre-workout cup improves endurance, power output and how hard effort feels - one of the most replicated findings in sports science.
- **Near-zero calories.** ~2-5 kcal per cup, so it's a good swap for sugary drinks when managing weight.
- **Appetite.** A mild, short-lived reduction in hunger.

## Associated (Population Studies, Not Proof)

- Lower risk of **type 2 diabetes**.
- Lower rates of **fatty liver** and liver fibrosis.
- Lower **all-cause mortality** in moderate drinkers.
- Possible links to lower risk of Parkinson's and some liver cancers.

These come from observational data - people who drink moderate coffee tend to do better, but the studies can't prove coffee is the cause.

## Overstated

- "Boosts metabolism to burn fat" - real but small (a few dozen calories) and fades with tolerance.
- "Detoxes" or "cleanses" - no.
- "Fat-burning coffee" products - the coffee isn't doing anything a normal black coffee wouldn't.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is black coffee good for you?**
In moderation - 2-4 cups a day, before mid-afternoon - yes, for most healthy adults. It's calorie-free, sharpens alertness, and is linked to lower rates of several chronic conditions in population studies. It's not a cure or a supplement, just a reasonably good daily habit.

**Is black coffee good for your heart?**
Moderate intake is generally associated with neutral-to-lower cardiovascular risk in most studies, not harm. That said, caffeine can raise heart rate and blood pressure short-term, so anyone with an existing heart condition or arrhythmia should check with a doctor about how much fits their situation.

**Is black coffee good for your kidneys?**
For healthy kidneys, normal coffee intake isn't a problem and some studies associate it with lower kidney stone risk, likely from increased fluid intake. If you have existing kidney disease, caffeine and fluid intake should be managed with your doctor rather than adjusted based on general advice.

**Is black coffee good for your skin?**
Coffee contains antioxidants that have some general skin-health associations, but there's no strong evidence that drinking it changes your skin in a noticeable way. Dehydration from too much caffeine (or too little water alongside it) can work against you, so balance it with plenty of water.

**Does black coffee help with acidity?**
Not usually - coffee is acidic and can trigger or worsen acid reflux and heartburn for many people, especially on an empty stomach. If you're prone to acidity, black coffee is more likely to aggravate it than help; a milder roast, smaller cup, or drinking it after food can reduce the effect.

**Is drinking black coffee for weight loss effective?**
It can support a weight-loss plan indirectly - it's calorie-free (unlike a milky, sugared coffee), has a mild appetite-suppressing effect, and gives a small, short-lived metabolic bump. It's not a fat-burner on its own; it works alongside diet and exercise, not instead of them.
