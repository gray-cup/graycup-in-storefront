Every flavoured latte follows the same basic formula: strong coffee, hot milk, and one flavour element - a syrup, a spice, or a spread - stirred in before or after. Once you have a good coffee base, the flavour part takes thirty seconds. Here are the most popular variations and how to make them.

## The Base Recipe

For any flavour below: pull a shot of espresso or make a small, concentrated filter coffee decoction (about 60-90ml), steam or heat 200-250ml of milk, then combine with your flavouring of choice. Adjust sugar to taste - most syrups already carry sweetness.

## Caramel Latte

Stir 1-2 tablespoons of caramel syrup into your coffee before adding milk. For a **salted caramel latte**, add a small pinch of sea salt on top of the caramel, or use a pre-made salted caramel syrup. Finish with a light caramel drizzle if you want the cafe look.

## Hazelnut Latte

Hazelnut syrup (1-2 tbsp) stirred into the coffee, topped with steamed milk. For a **toffee nut latte**, combine hazelnut syrup with a small amount of caramel or toffee syrup - it's essentially a hazelnut-caramel hybrid.

## Nutella Latte

Melt a heaped teaspoon of Nutella into your hot espresso or decoction, stirring until fully dissolved, then add steamed milk. This gives you both the hazelnut and chocolate notes in one step - closer to a mocha than a plain hazelnut latte.

## Coconut Latte

Swap some or all of the dairy milk for coconut milk, or add a small amount of coconut syrup to a regular latte. Coconut milk froths differently from dairy - it won't hold as stiff a foam, so expect a thinner top layer.

## Cinnamon Latte

Stir a quarter teaspoon of ground cinnamon into the coffee itself (it dissolves better in hot liquid than sprinkled on foam), or use a cinnamon syrup. A dash of cinnamon on top of the foam is mostly for looks and aroma rather than flavour strength.

## Choco Latte / Mocha Latte / Cafe Mocha Latte

These names are largely interchangeable for the same drink: espresso or strong coffee, chocolate syrup or cocoa powder, and milk. For a richer version, use melted dark chocolate instead of syrup. Latte and mocha differ only in that one - mocha always has chocolate, latte doesn't.

## Dolce Latte (Sweetened Milk Latte)

Note: "dolce latte" is also the name of a soft Italian blue cheese, unrelated to coffee - if you're searching for the drink, look for "dolce" (sweet) latte recipes that typically use condensed or sweetened milk rather than the cheese. A dolce-style latte just uses sweetened condensed milk in place of regular milk and sugar, giving a thicker, sweeter drink similar in spirit to a Spanish latte.

## Oat Milk Latte (Dairy-Free)

Oat milk froths well and holds foam reasonably close to dairy, making it one of the better plant-based options for lattes. Use a barista-blend oat milk if you can find one - regular oat milk (meant for drinking, not steaming) can split or thin out when heated. All the flavour variations above work the same way with oat milk substituted in.

## Getting the Coffee Base Right

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I make flavoured lattes without an espresso machine?**
Yes - a strong filter coffee decoction, moka pot, or even concentrated instant coffee works as the base. The flavour and milk steps are identical.

**Which flavoured latte has the least sugar?**
Cinnamon latte, since it uses spice rather than syrup. Coconut and Nutella versions tend to be sweetest because of added sugar in the coconut syrup or Nutella itself.

**What's the difference between latte and mocha again?**
Chocolate - a mocha is a latte with chocolate added, nothing else changes.
