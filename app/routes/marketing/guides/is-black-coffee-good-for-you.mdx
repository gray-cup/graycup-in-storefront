For most healthy adults, moderate black coffee - around 2 to 4 cups a day - is not just harmless but associated with some benefits in large population studies. It's close to calorie-free and contains hundreds of plant compounds, including chlorogenic acids and antioxidants. This isn't medical advice, and coffee affects people differently, but here's what the evidence generally supports.

## What the Research Associates With Coffee

- **Alertness, reaction time and short-term focus** - well established, from caffeine.
- **Better exercise performance** - endurance and perceived effort improve with caffeine pre-workout.
- **Lower risk of type 2 diabetes** - consistent in observational data, mechanism not fully clear.
- **Better liver markers** - regular coffee is linked to lower rates of fatty liver and liver fibrosis.
- **Lower all-cause mortality** in moderate drinkers, in several large cohort studies.

These are associations, not guarantees, and they apply to black coffee - adding sugar and cream changes the picture.

## The Downsides

- **Sleep.** Caffeine has a ~5-hour half-life; afternoon coffee costs sleep quality.
- **Anxiety and palpitations** in sensitive people or at high doses.
- **Pregnancy** - most guidance caps caffeine at ~200 mg/day.
- **Acid reflux** - coffee can worsen it for some.

## Keep It Simple

Drink it black, keep it to 2-4 cups, finish by mid-afternoon, and skip it if it makes you jittery or wrecks your sleep.
