Light roast is stopped soon after first crack. It keeps the most of what the bean brought from the farm - fruit, floral and tea-like notes, brighter acidity, a lighter body. It's the roast for tasting where a coffee is from, and it's best brewed black in a pour over or filter.

## What Light Roast Is Good For

- **Pour over and filter** - methods that show clarity and aroma.
- **Black coffee** - no milk to hide the delicate notes.
- **Single origins** - the whole point is origin character.
- **Lower bitterness** - lighter roasts are less roasty-bitter (though more acidic).

It's less forgiving: light roasts can taste sour or grassy if under-extracted, so grind a little finer and use hotter water than you would for a dark roast.

## Brewing Light Roast

Water at 93-96 C, a slightly finer grind, and a full 3-minute pour over. If it tastes sour or thin, grind finer or extend the brew - that's under-extraction, not the coffee being bad.
