A latte (short for the Italian *caffè latte*, "milk coffee") is one or two shots of espresso topped with steamed milk and a thin layer of milk foam - usually about a centimetre. That's it. What makes a latte a latte isn't a flavour, it's a ratio: a lot of milk, a little foam, and coffee that's strong enough to still taste like coffee underneath it. A latte is milkier and less foamy than a cappuccino, which is why it feels smoother and less "frothy" in the mouth.

If you've ever stood at a counter unsure what to order, the confusion usually isn't about what a latte *is* - it's about how many things get called a latte. This guide clears that up.

## Latte vs Cappuccino vs Mocha vs Flat White

People use these names loosely, but each one is a specific ratio of the same three ingredients: espresso, steamed milk, and foam.

- **Latte** - mostly steamed milk, a thin foam cap. Smooth, mellow, milk-forward.
- **Cappuccino** - equal-ish parts espresso, steamed milk, and foam. Thicker foam, more concentrated coffee flavour per sip.
- **Mocha** - a latte with chocolate added. Sweeter, dessert-like.
- **Flat white** - similar milk-to-coffee ratio to a latte but made with "microfoam" (very finely textured, almost no visible foam layer) and usually a slightly stronger coffee base.

If you want a full side-by-side on the sweeter, cocoa-forward version, see our guide on [black coffee vs milk coffee](/guides/black-coffee-vs-milk-coffee) for how milk changes body and bitterness generally.

## Common Types of Latte

- **Iced latte** - espresso poured over ice with cold milk, no steaming involved.
- **Flavoured latte** - vanilla, caramel, hazelnut or seasonal syrups added to the base.
- **Oat milk (or other plant milk) latte** - same method, a dairy-free milk that froths and steams differently (oat milk tends to foam well; almond milk less so).
- **Iced latte, cold** - just to be clear, "latte espresso" served cold is simply an iced latte; the espresso itself isn't brewed any differently.

## What a "Latte Lover" Actually Likes

If you gravitate toward lattes over black coffee or cappuccino, you're generally someone who wants coffee flavour without the intensity - milk mellows both the bitterness and the acidity of espresso. That's a legitimate preference, not a "lesser" one; it just means the coffee itself needs to be flavourful enough to still come through under all that milk, which is where the bean matters more than people expect.

## How to Make a Latte at Home Without an Espresso Machine

You don't need an espresso machine to get close to a real latte:

1. **Make a strong coffee base.** A moka pot gives you something close to espresso strength. No moka pot? Brew a South Indian filter coffee decoction, or a very strong pour-over/French press concentrate - anything strong enough that it won't disappear under milk.
2. **Steam or froth your milk.** A milk frother, French press (pump the plunger up and down in hot milk), or even a tightly sealed jar shaken and microwaved will get you steamed, slightly foamed milk.
3. **Combine.** Pour the coffee base first, then the milk, holding back the foam with a spoon until the end, then spoon it on top.
4. **Ratio.** Roughly 1 part strong coffee to 3-4 parts milk is a good starting point for a latte-style cup.

The coffee you start with matters more here than the frothing technique - a decoction made from a proper filter coffee blend, or a French-press concentrate from a full-bodied Arabica-Robusta blend, holds up under milk far better than weak drip coffee.
