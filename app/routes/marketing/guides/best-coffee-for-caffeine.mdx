Three things decide how much caffeine ends up in your cup: the bean, the roast, and how you brew it.

- **Bean:** Robusta carries about 2,400 mg of caffeine per 100 g against Arabica's ~1,300 mg - close to double. A higher-Robusta blend means more caffeine, cup for cup.
- **Roast:** by weight, dark roast has slightly *less* caffeine than light (roasting burns a little off), but the difference is small. Roast affects taste far more than caffeine.
- **Brew:** more grounds, less water, longer contact, and no dilution afterward. A strong South Indian decoction or a concentrated brew delivers much more than a weak drip.

The numbers below assume a 30 g dose of grounds per cup - a strong brew.

## How to Brew for Maximum Caffeine

Use a 1:12 coffee-to-water ratio instead of the usual 1:16. Brew a concentrate and drink it neat or with a splash of hot water - not a full dilution. For filter coffee, make a thick decoction and go easy on the water.

## A Limit Worth Knowing

Around 400 mg of caffeine a day (3-4 strong cups) is the commonly cited safe ceiling for healthy adults; less if you're pregnant or caffeine-sensitive. Past that you get jitters and worse sleep, not more energy.
