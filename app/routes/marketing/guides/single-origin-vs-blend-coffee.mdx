A single-origin coffee comes from one place - one country, region, estate or even one lot. A blend mixes beans from different origins (and often different processes or roasts) to hit a target flavour. Neither is better; they're built for different things.

## Single Origin

- **Shows terroir** - the flavour of a specific place and process.
- **Varies** - a great estate has good years and less good years; the next bag won't taste identical.
- **Best for:** pour over, filter, black coffee, tasting and comparing.
- **Usually:** lighter to medium roast to preserve character.

## Blend

- **Consistent** - roasters adjust the recipe so it tastes the same year-round.
- **Balanced** - strengths of one bean cover another's gaps (e.g. Robusta body under Arabica aroma).
- **Best for:** espresso, milk coffee, South Indian filter, an everyday house coffee.
- **Cheaper**, usually.

## Which to Buy

If you want to explore and taste differences, buy single origins - ideally via a sampler. If you want a reliable daily cup, especially with milk or espresso, buy a blend.
