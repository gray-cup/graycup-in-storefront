When you add milk and sugar, you can get away with a cheap, over-roasted, high-Robusta coffee - the dairy covers the bitterness. Drink it black and there's nowhere to hide. Coffee that's genuinely good black shares a few traits: mostly or all Arabica, a lighter-to-medium roast, a clean process, and low bitterness.

## What Makes a Coffee Easy to Drink Black

- **Arabica, not Robusta.** Robusta is roughly twice as bitter and much harsher plain.
- **Washed or honey process.** Cleaner, more defined, fewer rough edges than a heavy natural.
- **Medium roast or lighter.** Dark roasts add roasty bitterness that milk normally masks.
- **Fresh.** Stale coffee tastes flat and papery, which is worse without milk to distract you.

## Brewing Tips for Black Coffee

- **Grind fresh** if possible - stale grounds taste worse without milk.
- **Water at 90-96 C**, not fully boiling, to avoid scorching.
- **Start at 1:16** coffee to water and adjust. Too strong reads as bitter; too weak reads as sour.
- **If it's bitter,** grind coarser or use cooler water. If it's sour, grind finer or brew longer.
